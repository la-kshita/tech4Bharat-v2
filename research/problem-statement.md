# Problem statement and attribution

## Challenge context

The Tech4Bharat 2026 JanAI Mass Entrepreneurship Challenge's Problem Statement 4.2 describes how manual inspection can let defects pass through small manufacturing units and limit their access to larger supply chains. It says manual inspection misses 10–15% of defects, while industrial machine-vision systems can be costly and require specialist support.

The brief describes line workers checking by eye or sampling, defects being discovered after dispatch, and quality records often being thin or missing. It connects the problem to rejected consignments, chargebacks, and reduced access to buyers that require evidence of consistent quality.

**Attribution:** the 10–15% figure above is from the challenge brief. It has not been measured by this project and should not be presented as a local enterprise baseline.

## Why begin with PCB assembly inspection?

The project is deliberately narrowing a broad inspection challenge to one populated PCB model and revision. This creates a bounded pilot question: can a worker capture a usable image of a known board, compare visible areas with an approved reference, review possible flags, and record what was actually inspected?

The choice is a starting scope, not a claim that PCBs are the most affected sector or that one model will generalize to other boards. PCB assembly inspection also differs from bare-board copper-pattern inspection. Visible photos cannot verify hidden solder joints, electrical function, component values, or defects outside the selected view.

## Field questions in the challenge brief

- Which defects matter to the buyer, and how are they found today?
- How often are consignments rejected?
- What proof of quality does the buyer ask for?
- What are the lighting and workspace conditions?
- How often does the product change?
- Who labels defects, and how much time can they spend?

These are discovery questions. This archive does not claim that target enterprises or buyers have answered them.
